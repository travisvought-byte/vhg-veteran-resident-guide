What this benefit is

VA pension is a monthly payment for wartime veterans, and for their surviving spouses, who have limited income and savings. Aid and Attendance and Housebound are extra amounts added to the pension for people who need help with daily life or who cannot leave home. VA bases the payment on the gap between a yearly limit set by Congress and the income VA counts.

For someone in a nursing home or assisted living, the cost of care is often subtracted from income. That is why many families who think they earn too much still qualify.

Who can qualify

The veteran

  • Was not dishonorably discharged.
  • Served during a wartime period: at least 90 days of active duty with at least 1 day in wartime if service began before September 8, 1980. Later enlisted service generally needs 24 months, or the full period called to duty, with at least 1 day in wartime.
  • And at least one of these is true: age 65 or older; permanent and total disability; a patient in a nursing home for long-term care because of a disability; or receiving Social Security Disability Insurance or Supplemental Security Income.
  • Family income and net worth are under the limits below.

Wartime periods VA uses: World War II (December 7, 1941, to December 31, 1946); Korean conflict (June 27, 1950, to January 31, 1955); Vietnam War era (November 1, 1955, to May 7, 1975, for service in the Republic of Vietnam; August 5, 1964, to May 7, 1975, for service anywhere else); Gulf War (August 2, 1990, to a date not yet set). Earlier periods are on VA's eligibility page.

The surviving spouse

  • Has not remarried since the veteran died.
  • Lived with the veteran from marriage until death. VA can accept time apart for health, work or the veteran's misconduct.
  • The veteran's service meets the wartime rule above and the discharge was not dishonorable.
  • Income and net worth are under the limits.

Aid and Attendance or Housebound

Aid and Attendance can be added if at least one is true: the person needs another person's help with daily activities such as bathing, eating or dressing; must stay in bed, or spend much of the day in bed, because of illness; is in a nursing home because of lost mental or physical abilities related to a disability; or has very limited eyesight (5/200 or less in both eyes, or a visual field of 5 degrees or less).

Housebound can be added if the person spends most of the time at home because of a permanent disability. A person cannot get both.

The limits and rates (as of November 23, 2026)

Net worth limit: $163,699 for December 1, 2025, to November 30, 2026. VA adjusts its limits and rates every December 1; check VA's rates page after that date for the new figures.

Net worth is everything owned, minus debts, plus yearly income. It does not include the home where the person lives most of the time, the car, or basic household items. A married veteran's net worth includes the spouse's.

How the payment is figured: the yearly maximum minus the income VA counts. Example using VA's table: a veteran with no spouse or child who qualifies for Aid and Attendance has a maximum of $29,093. If VA counts $20,000 of yearly income after medical costs are subtracted, the pension is $9,093 a year, paid monthly (about $757 a month).

How nursing home and care costs count

VA subtracts medical expenses you pay yourself and are not paid back for from the income it counts. Only the part above 5% of the yearly maximum is subtracted: $872 for a veteran with no dependents, $1,141 for a veteran with 1 dependent, $584 for a surviving spouse with no child.

  • Nursing homes: payments to a nursing home count as medical expenses, including the meals and lodging the home charges.
  • Assisted living and other care facilities: payments for care count. Meals and lodging also count when the facility provides or arranges health care or custodial care for the person, or when a physician, physician assistant or nurse practitioner states in writing that the person needs to live in that kind of setting to receive care.
  • Home care: paid help with daily activities can count, with rules about who the aide is.
  • Insurance premiums, including Medicare Part B and Part D and long-term care insurance, count.
  • Whatever Medicaid, insurance or anyone else pays does not count. Only out-of-pocket costs do.

Forms that usually go together for a nursing home resident: the pension application (VA Form 21P-527EZ for veterans; 21P-534EZ for surviving spouses); VA Form 21-2680, the exam a medical examiner completes; VA Form 21-0779, which the nursing home completes; and VA Form 21P-8416, the medical expense report.

If Medicaid pays for the nursing home: the $90 rule

A veteran with no spouse or dependent child, or a surviving spouse with no child, who is in a Medicaid-covered nursing home receives no more than $90 a month in VA pension, starting after the month Medicaid payments begin.

The nursing home cannot count that $90 toward the cost of care. The resident keeps it for personal needs. In some states it is more than the personal spending money Medicaid allows.

This can matter even for someone who already gets VA disability compensation or Dependency and Indemnity Compensation (DIC). If the facility takes most of that payment for care, applying for pension can leave more spending money: when VA finds a person eligible for both, it pays pension at the $90 rate instead. Tell VA when Medicaid begins paying.

A veteran with a spouse or dependent child is not limited to $90 by this rule. Before changing any benefit, ask an accredited representative and the facility's business office to look at VA and Medicaid together.

The 3-year look-back on gifts and transfers

When someone files, VA reviews assets given away or sold for less than they were worth in the 3 years before the claim. If those assets would have put net worth over the limit, VA can set a penalty period of up to 5 years with no pension. VA's penalty rate is $2,874. The look-back never reaches back before October 18, 2018.

Medicaid has its own, separate look-back. A transfer that seems to help with one program can hurt with the other.

Get free, accredited help

Only people VA has accredited may help prepare a VA claim. Free help comes from accredited Veterans Service Organization representatives and county veterans service officers. Accredited attorneys and claims agents may charge a fee only after VA has decided the first claim, and only with a signed fee agreement on file with VA.

  • Find a VA-accredited representative on VA.gov.
  • Find your county veterans office in this guide.
  • VA benefits line: 800-827-1000.

Be careful with pension “advisors”

VA's Inspector General warns about “pension poaching.” Be wary of anyone who:

  • promises the person will qualify;
  • tells you to move money into a trust, buy an annuity, or leave out income or expenses so the person qualifies;
  • charges an upfront fee or a percentage of the benefit to file a claim (free help is available from accredited helpers);
  • presses for bank or credit card details; or
  • wants VA deposits sent to an account that does not belong to the veteran or spouse.

These sales often happen at senior centers and assisted living communities. Moving assets can trigger VA's penalty period and Medicaid's. Report a pension scheme to the VA Office of Inspector General: 800-488-8244 or online. Someone who is not accredited and is helping with VA claims can be reported to VA's Office of General Counsel.

What to do next

If you are the veteran

Ask the social worker or your county veterans office to check whether you qualify. Bring your DD214 or tell them it is missing; they can request it.

If you are the spouse

If your spouse is in care and you are at home, VA counts you as a dependent, so the higher yearly maximum for a veteran with a dependent is used; your income and savings count too. Ask before you sell or move anything.

If you are an adult son or daughter

Gather the DD214, a list of income and savings, and a year of nursing home bills. Do not move money or sign up with a paid advisor until an accredited representative has looked.

If you are a social worker or facility staff

Complete VA Form 21-0779 when asked. Flag residents who are wartime veterans or surviving spouses with private-pay or Medicaid stays, and refer them to the county veterans office. For Medicaid residents with no spouse, ask about the $90 rate.
