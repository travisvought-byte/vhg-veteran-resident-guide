FOR VETERANS IN CARE AND THEIR FAMILIES

Abuse, neglect or money being taken: where to report

If a veteran or older adult is being hurt, neglected, or robbed of money or benefits, you can report it. Every state has Adult Protective Services. Find your state below.

What counts, and who can report

Anyone can report. You do not need proof, only a real concern. Many states, including Ohio, let you report without giving your name.

  • Abuse: hitting, rough handling, threats, yelling, sexual contact without consent, or keeping someone locked in or isolated.
  • Neglect: a caregiver not providing food, medicine, hygiene or needed care, or a person no longer able to care for themselves (self-neglect).
  • Exploitation: money, property, a pension or VA benefit taken, misused or signed away under pressure.

Where to report

  • The person lives at home or with family: call Adult Protective Services (APS) in that state. Find your state below.
  • The person lives in a nursing home or assisted living: tell the administrator, and call the long-term care ombudsman, a free advocate for residents. The state health or licensing agency also takes complaints about care, and in some states APS takes reports about care settings too; the state pages below say where to call. This guide lists each state's ombudsman under “Concerns about care or rights”: Ohio, or choose your state from the list below.
  • Money was stolen or a crime happened: also call the local police.

Not sure where to start? The Eldercare Locator, run by the U.S. Administration for Community Living, can connect you to local help: 800-677-1116.

In Ohio

Ohio Adult Protective Services: 855-644-6277 (855-OHIO-APS), or report online at aps.jfs.ohio.gov. To report anonymously, call. The Ohio Department of Job and Family Services oversees APS for adults 60 and older, and each county department of job and family services must take reports 24 hours a day, 7 days a week.

Abuse or neglect in a nursing home or other care facility: the Ohio Attorney General takes reports of patient abuse and neglect online. For quality-of-care problems, the Attorney General's page points to the Ohio Department of Health: 800-342-0553.

Resident rights and care concerns: see the ombudsman contacts in this guide's Ohio section; choose a county there for the local ombudsman.

When money or benefits are being taken

  • Signs: unpaid nursing home bills when income should cover them; new “friends” or helpers handling money; missing checks, cards or valuables; sudden changes to a will, deed or bank account; a resident with no spending money.
  • Call the bank's fraud line, report to APS, and call the police if money was stolen.
  • Pressure to move assets so someone “qualifies” for VA pension is a known scheme. See the warning on the pension page.

If a VA fiduciary is misusing VA benefits

When VA decides a beneficiary cannot manage VA payments, it appoints a fiduciary to handle those payments only. VA oversees fiduciaries with field exams, accountings and onsite reviews.

If you believe a fiduciary is misusing the money, call the VA Fiduciary program: 888-407-0144, and report it to the VA Office of Inspector General: 800-488-8244 or online. The Inspector General also asks people to report anyone trying to redirect VA deposits to an account controlled by a caregiver, power of attorney or claims helper. You can report to APS as well.

What to do next

If you are the veteran

You have the right to be safe and to control your own money. You can call APS yourself, or ask the ombudsman to help you raise a concern without your name attached.

If you are the spouse

If you think your spouse is being harmed in a facility, write down what you saw, the date and who was there. Then call the ombudsman and the state number below.

If you are an adult son or daughter

If a sibling, friend or new helper controls the money and bills are going unpaid, report it. A power of attorney does not allow anyone to take money for themselves.

If you are a social worker or facility staff

State law often requires care staff to report suspected abuse. Follow your facility policy and your state law, and do not wait for proof.

Crisis and urgent lines

Veterans Crisis Line: call 988 and press 1, or text 838255. If someone is in danger right now, call 911.
