## County boundaries and contact selection

All 100 names come from the official NCDHHS local DSS directory embedded map and match the state veterans and aging maps exactly. Each entry is a county; there is no North Carolina County or independent-city collision. The older veterans PDF has 98 county headings, omits Forsyth and Lincoln, duplicates some outreach locations and misspells Swain as “Swaine.” Lincoln uses its current county website. Forsyth uses the explicitly assigned Clemmons state center.

89 entries use county offices; 11 use the current state map and center phone table: Bertie, Camden, Caswell, Currituck, Forsyth, Graham, Lenoir, Pasquotank, Swain, Tyrrell and Wilson. These are labeled regional contacts, not county offices. Bertie’s old county phone was not independently resolved; its current state-assigned Elizabeth City contact is published instead. County offices drawn only from the 2024–2025 PDF retain that period and a call-before-travel note. Durham’s state map names a Durham VAMC center absent from the 12-center phone table; Durham uses its explicitly published county office phone, not an invented regional phone.

Aging uses 16 county groups (A–G and J–R); the regional ombudsman directory has the same county sets. Aging phones are agency/general routing lines from the embedded state aging map. Ombudsman contacts are separate program routes from the complete state-linked May 2026 DOCX, never inferred from the aging general line. Some agency switchboards serve both functions. Foothills was formerly Isothermal; Central Pines was formerly Triangle J; names are updated from the newer state directory while source-name differences remain explicit. Eastern Band of Cherokee Indians is mentioned in aging Region A; the veterans PDF also lists EBCI and Lumbee tribal contacts. Tribal nations are not additional counties and no county-wide tribal eligibility or territorial assignment is inferred. Military-installation eligibility and service boundaries are not inferred from nearby counties. No reviewed source assigns a county to an out-of-state agency.
