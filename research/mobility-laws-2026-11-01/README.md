# State-by-state disability advocacy research matrix

As of 2026-11-02. Research pass 4: 50 states and DC tracked; selected evidence for 51 states; 86 observations and 75 reviewed primary-publisher sources. 9 issue families; federal observations remain separate. Public site content is unchanged.

This matrix supports advocacy: compare protections, exclusions and accountability, then identify policy changes. It does not route individual service requests. A reviewed cell means at least one bounded observation, not a complete legal review. Unknown never means no right. Source types and version limits remain visible.

**Repair-period backup payment:** Decision-makers: State Medicaid agencies and state legislatures. Limits: Payer and facility scopes differ; managed-care practice and suitable inventory remain unmeasured.

**Temporary-equipment trigger:** Decision-makers: State legislatures and consumer-protection regulators. Limits: Different day units, alternate triggers and warranty scopes prevent a simple numeric ranking.

**Equipment definitions:** Decision-makers: State legislatures. Limits: A narrower cited law does not prove the state lacks other protections.

**Free-donation exclusion:** Decision-makers: Illinois legislature. Limits: Other rights remain possible; transferable warranties, donor duties and effects need research.

**Suitable backup equipment:** Decision-makers: Legislatures, Medicaid agencies and manufacturers. Limits: Warranty loaner definitions, Medicaid inventory duties and personal-backup funding are different mechanisms. Oregon indexed text needs session reconciliation. No delivery-outcome ranking.

**Response versus continuity:** Decision-makers: State legislatures and consumer-protection regulators. Limits: Response or ordering deadlines are not repair-completion guarantees.

**Community-support eligibility boundaries:** Decision-makers: State disability and Medicaid agencies; state legislatures. Limits: Different programs, not a whole-state eligibility ranking. Exclusion from one program does not prove no other support exists.

**Warranty remedy clocks are not repair deadlines:** Decision-makers: State legislatures and consumer-protection authorities. Limits: New Jersey is agency guidance; Michigan compilation is dated 2025 and needs currency review. Definitions, warranty windows and notice duties differ. Not a ranking or loaner guarantee.

**Fund continuity through suitable backup equipment:** Decision-makers: State Medicaid agencies and legislatures. Limits: Different benefits and eligibility groups: a temporary rental and repair of a retained backup are not interchangeable. New York storage conditions may constrain access; outcomes and cost-effectiveness unmeasured.

## Federal layer

- **Insurance and public-payer approvals:** Medicare Part B wheelchair/scooter coverage has medical necessity, examination/prescription and supplier requirements; certain power chairs require prior approval. Not a Medicaid, private-insurer or universal repair-access rule; distinguish Medicare Advantage administration. [us-medicare](https://www.medicare.gov/coverage/wheelchairs-scooters)
- **Public transportation access:** FTA accepts public-transit discrimination complaints and encourages contacting the local transit provider first. FTA guidance recommends filing within 180 days; do not substitute a policy letter for a complaint process. [us-transit](https://www.transit.dot.gov/regulations-and-guidance/civil-rights-ada/file-complaint-fta)
- **Community living and personal assistance:** DOJ describes conditional rights to community integration rather than unnecessary institutional segregation. Does not guarantee a specific waiver slot, personal-assistance hours or chosen equipment. [us-community](https://www.ada.gov/topics/community-integration/)

## Inference ledger

### oh-downtime-gap

**Inference:** Warranty-backed loaners and Medicaid payment restrictions may leave a continuity gap outside the warranty route.
**Premises:** oh-loaner-1, oh-payer-1.
**Next action:** Research who supplies and funds backup mobility outside qualifying warranty cases; ask ODM about an interim-equipment policy.
**Limit:** Does not establish that every person lacks a backup option or that a particular denial is unlawful.

### enforcement-before-newlaw

**Inference:** Some repair-delay problems may be enforcement or awareness issues, not missing-law issues.
**Premises:** oh-loaner-1, ky-loaner-1, ct-timely-1.
**Next action:** Ask about warranty status and repair dates, then verify complaint route before generating a new-law request.
**Limit:** No individual violation or deadline calculation is inferred.

### repair-access-not-coverage

**Inference:** Access to manufacturer resources does not by itself establish an insurance payment entitlement.
**Premises:** wa-repair-1, nv-repair-1, us-payer-1.
**Next action:** Separate parts/tool refusal from payer denial and route each to the responsible actor.
**Limit:** No conclusion that more repair rights reduce cost or delays without outcome data.

### parts-vs-provider

**Inference:** Manufacturer-access duties and dealer service-time duties address distinct bottlenecks.
**Premises:** wa-repair-1, ct-timely-1.
**Next action:** Offer separate advocacy requests for parts access, service response/parts ordering, and continuity equipment.
**Limit:** Cross-state comparison suggests policy options; it is not a ranking of state accessibility.
