OTHER-THAN-HONORABLE, GENERAL AND BAD-CONDUCT DISCHARGES

Which board, which form

Two kinds of military boards can change a discharge. Which one you go to depends on how long ago you got out and what kind of discharge you have.

Rough night? Call 988 and press 1, or text 838255. A VA medical center will see you in an emergency whatever your discharge.

Two kinds of boards

Discharge Review Board (DRB): DD Form 293

  • You must apply within 15 years of the date of discharge (10 U.S.C. 1553).
  • It reviews whether the discharge was proper and whether it was fair (propriety and equity). It can change the character of service, the narrative reason, and the separation and reentry codes.
  • It cannot review a discharge from a general court-martial. For a discharge from a special court-martial it can upgrade only as clemency.
  • Propriety means the rules were followed. One example: since 2009, the law requires a medical exam for PTSD or TBI before certain administrative other-than-honorable separations of members who deployed (check your file, 10 U.S.C. 1177).
  • It cannot change a discharge to or from a medical or disability discharge, and it cannot reinstate you (NDRB).

Board for Correction of Military or Naval Records (BCMR or BCNR): DD Form 149

  • The highest administrative review in each service. It can correct any error or injustice in your record, including a discharge.
  • It can grant clemency for a discharge from any court-martial, general court-martial included (Wilkie memo).
  • The law asks you to file within three years of discovering the error or injustice, and lets the board excuse a late filing in the interest of justice (10 U.S.C. 1552(b)). For PTSD and TBI cases, DoD directed the boards to waive that limit (Carson memo). The Coast Guard board asks you to explain any delay.
  • If you are still inside the 15-year window, the boards expect you to go to the Discharge Review Board first (Army, Coast Guard, Navy).

How a Discharge Review Board review works

The Navy’s board, which also hears Marine Corps cases, spells out its process (NDRB). The other review boards work along similar lines; check your board’s page.

  • You get two looks: one documentary record review and one personal appearance hearing. The board recommends the record review first, then a hearing if you do not get full relief. If you ask for the hearing first, you lose the record review.
  • Hearings are held at the Washington Navy Yard. You can appear in person at your own expense, by phone or by video.
  • Timing: within about 8 weeks you should get a letter. If accepted, it comes with a docket number. Put your name and docket number on anything you send later.
  • Evidence must arrive before the review or hearing. Anything that comes after is not considered.
  • A lawyer is not required, but the board itself says counsel often helps people present a coherent, persuasive case and improves the odds. Many legal aid and veterans organizations help free (free help).
  • Make the case about what happened and fairness. The board cannot upgrade just so you can get VA benefits or a better job, or just because time has passed.
  • If you are denied after both reviews, or the 15 years run out, the next step is the Board for Correction. If you were discharged on or after December 20, 2019 and have gone through both boards, there is one more level, the DoD Discharge Appeal Review Board.

Filling out the form

  • Download the current form from the DoD forms site: DD-293 or DD-149. The BCNR warns that older versions may not be read by its system.
  • Sign and date it. The veteran signs, unless the veteran has died or been found legally incompetent, with proof.
  • Say exactly what you want changed: character of service, narrative reason, separation code, reentry code. Then explain why. On the DD-293 your reasons go in block 20; on the DD-149, block 15 (Air Force boards).
  • On a DD-149, give the date you discovered the error or injustice, and if it was more than three years ago, explain the delay (Coast Guard BCMR).
  • Attach copies of your evidence, never originals. Signed statements from witnesses help.
  • Submit once. Sending the same application by mail, fax and email slows it down and can restart the process (BCNR).
  • Keep your address current with the board while your case is open.

If PTSD, TBI, military sexual trauma or another mental health condition played a part, say so plainly in the application. That is what triggers liberal consideration (next page).
