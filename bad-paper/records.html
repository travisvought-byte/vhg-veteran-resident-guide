OTHER-THAN-HONORABLE, GENERAL AND BAD-CONDUCT DISCHARGES

Get your records first

Every route here runs on paper: your DD-214, your full personnel file and your medical records. Requests take time, so order them before anything else.

Rough night? Call 988 and press 1, or text 838255. A VA medical center will see you in an emergency whatever your discharge.

What you need

  • Your DD-214. The Navy’s review board asks for member copy 4 with the application (NDRB).
  • Your complete personnel file (the Official Military Personnel File). This is where the separation paperwork, counselings, nonjudicial punishment and evaluations are. You need it to see what the command relied on.
  • Your service treatment records: sick call, mental health visits, injuries.
  • Any VA records if you have been treated at VA or filed a claim before.
  • Post-service records that show who you are now: treatment, jobs, school, certificates.

Order from the National Archives

The National Personnel Records Center provides DD-214s and military personnel and medical record information free to veterans and next of kin when the discharge was less than 62 years ago. It notes that some companies charge for DD-214 copies it provides free (National Archives).

Online: eVetRecs

Start at vetrecs.archives.gov. You verify your identity with ID.me when you submit a new request and when you get electronic responses.

Paper: SF-180

Download the SF-180 (there is a fillable version), print, sign, and mail or fax it as the form directs. Use one form per person. Next of kin of a deceased veteran send proof of death (National Archives).

Ask for the complete personnel file, not only the DD-214. The DD-214 is the document most people request, but a discharge upgrade turns on the paperwork behind it.

To check a request, the Archives has an online status page, and its customer service line is 314-801-0800; it notes its peak calling hours are weekdays from 10 a.m. to 3 p.m. Central (National Archives). Originals you send are not returned, so send copies.

Where your service treatment records went

In the 1990s the services stopped filing health records with the personnel file, and for years they sent them to VA instead. If you separated in these windows, your service treatment records went to VA’s Records Management Center (National Archives):

Ohio: the state may have your discharge papers

The Ohio Department of Veterans Services records discharge papers from the early to mid-1990s to the present for veterans who enlisted from Ohio and asked that copies be sent to the state. Call its Records Office at 877-OHIO-VET (ODVS Benefits Resource Guide). It can be a faster way to get a DD-214 copy while the full file is on order.

Find an error? That is evidence

Read the file closely: missing deployments or awards, wrong dates, steps in the separation that should have happened and did not. A record error can support a request for correction or upgrade on its own. Mark it and show it to whoever is helping you.

One step to check if you deployed: since a 2009 law, a service member who deployed overseas in support of a contingency operation within the previous 24 months (or, since a 2016 change, was sexually assaulted) and was diagnosed with, or reasonably alleged, PTSD or TBI must get a medical examination before an administrative separation under other than honorable conditions, including one in lieu of court-martial. The results must be reviewed before the separation is approved. It does not apply to courts-martial (10 U.S.C. 1177). If your file shows no such exam, that bears on whether the discharge was proper.

If a Discharge Review Board already decided your case, records you could not get at the time may count as new evidence (32 CFR 70.8, what new evidence means).
