What the packet is

A free paper list of the first offices that help veterans, surviving spouses, older adults and people with disabilities: the county veterans office, VA health care, the Area Agency on Aging, the long-term care ombudsman, Adult Protective Services, free legal help and crisis lines. It is made for people who do not use the internet or a smartphone.

Which packet to print

  • Ohio: one packet for each county, two pages.
  • Large print: the same Ohio county contacts in 18-point type or larger.
  • Other states and D.C.: statewide contacts plus a county-by-county table.

Get them at guide.vethomeguard.org/packets.html, or scan the code above.

Counseling for veterans and families

VA Vet Centers offer counseling at no cost to eligible veterans, service members and their families: 877-927-8387.

How to use it

  1. Ask about service as part of the person’s story. Veterans and surviving spouses may qualify for benefits they have not applied for.
  2. At the end of life, involve the veterans office early. The County Veterans Service Office can help families with burial benefits, a headstone or marker, a flag and survivor benefits.
  3. Listen for care concerns. If the person describes a problem with their care, the ombudsman in the packet works for the resident and follows the resident’s wishes.
  4. Give the packet to the family as well. Families often handle the calls. The packet works without a computer.
  5. Know the crisis line. Veterans, families and friends can call 988 and press 1, or text 838255, at any hour.

Privacy

The packet asks for nothing and records nothing. Veteran Home Guardians never sees who receives a packet. Each office explains its own rules when you call.

If a number here is wrong

Please tell us so we can fix it for everyone. On the website, tap Report a problem next to the listing, or email travis@vethomeguard.org and name the packet and the office. Do not include anyone’s private or medical details.
