State source summaries

State law notes so far: 17 states, checked November 1, 2026. Every state also gets federal information in the finder above. These notes do not decide anyone’s eligibility, and a missing topic means we have not researched it yet.

Ohio

Warranty protection for qualifying defects

In Ohio, a qualifying new assistive device, such as a wheelchair, must come with at least a one-year warranty.

Source: ORC 1345.91(B) (from the law or rule text) · Ohio assistive-device warranty · checked 2026-11-01

Fine print

At least a one-year express warranty covers qualifying assistive-device defects.

Who it covers: Covered consumer transactions and warranty defects

Whether this applies depends on your device, seller and situation. Open the source to check the details.

Loaners during qualifying warranty repairs

In Ohio, if a qualifying warranty repair takes more than 21 days, or the same problem has come back three or more times, you can be owed a comparable loaner.

Source: ORC 1345.91(C) (from the law or rule text) · Ohio assistive-device warranty · checked 2026-11-01

Fine print

Comparable loaner required for qualifying warranty repair when repair exceeds 21 days, or the same defect was returned on three or more previous occasions.

Who it covers: Manufacturer/supplier warranty repair, not every breakdown

Whether this applies depends on your device, seller and situation. Open the source to check the details.

Medicaid repair and temporary-equipment payment rules

Ohio Medicaid may need to confirm the need for some wheelchair part repairs (not routine wear items), and it does not pay for a loaner while your wheelchair is repaired.

Source: OAC 5160-10-16(C)(3) (from the law or rule text) · Ohio Medicaid wheelchair rule · checked 2026-11-01

Fine print

Component repairs may require need verification; wear-item repairs do not. Medicaid does not pay for temporary loaner equipment while a wheelchair is repaired.

Who it covers: Wheelchairs for community users and custom chairs for LTC residents as specified

Non-custom LTC chairs have a separate facility responsibility rule. Managed-care implementation not reconciled.

Home modifications through a waiver program

Ohio’s Home Care Waiver can pay for home modifications for people enrolled in the waiver, with approval.

Source: OAC 5160-46-06(A)(3) (from the law or rule text) · Ohio Home Care Waiver covered services · checked 2026-11-01

Fine print

Home modification is listed among Ohio Home Care Waiver services.

Who it covers: Eligible waiver participants; service authorization required

Whether this applies depends on your device, seller and situation. Open the source to check the details.

Vehicle modifications through a waiver program

Ohio’s Home Care Waiver lists vehicle modifications as a service for people enrolled in the waiver.

Source: OAC 5160-46-06(A)(12) (from the law or rule text) · Ohio Home Care Waiver covered services · checked 2026-11-01

Fine print

Vehicle modifications are listed among waiver services.

Who it covers: Eligible Ohio Home Care Waiver participants

Whether this applies depends on your device, seller and situation. Open the source to check the details.

Community integration and transition services

Ohio’s Home Care Waiver lists help with community living and moving out of a facility as services.

Source: OAC 5160-46-06(A)(4),(8) (from the law or rule text) · Ohio Home Care Waiver covered services · checked 2026-11-01

Fine print

Community integration and transition are listed waiver services.

Who it covers: Eligible Ohio Home Care Waiver participants

Whether this applies depends on your device, seller and situation. Open the source to check the details.

Pennsylvania

Motorized-wheelchair warranty remedies

In Pennsylvania, a new motorized wheelchair must have at least a one-year warranty. If a defect can’t be fixed after reasonable tries, you may be owed a replacement or refund.

Source: 1994 Act 117, sections 3-4 (from the law or rule text) · Motorized Wheelchair Warranty Act · checked 2026-11-01

Fine print

Minimum one-year warranty; qualifying unresolved defects can lead to replacement or refund after a reasonable repair attempt.

Who it covers: New motorized wheelchairs and covered consumers

Motorized scope; definition of reasonable repair attempt and consumer procedure must be retained. This is not independent parts access.

New York

Wheelchair warranty replacement or refund triggers

In New York, a new wheelchair has at least a one-year warranty. After three failed repair tries, or 30 total days out of service, you may be owed a replacement or refund.

Source: GBS 670*2(1)(k),(2),(3) (from the law or rule text) · Wheelchair warranty remedies · checked 2026-11-01

Fine print

One-year minimum wheelchair warranty; reasonable-repair definition includes at least three repair attempts with continuing defect, or at least 30 aggregate days out of service.

Who it covers: New wheelchair warranty nonconformities; definitions/exclusions apply

Threshold is a remedy trigger, not a universal repair-completion deadline.

Michigan

Where to check Medicaid repair policy

Michigan Medicaid publishes a wheelchair repair guide for equipment suppliers. Ask your supplier or plan which rules apply to your repair.

Source: MDHHS DME provider page (official program page) · Michigan medical supplier policy gateway · checked 2026-11-01

Fine print

MDHHS publishes a wheelchair repair/labor guide and states its provider manual controls policy discrepancies.

Who it covers: Michigan Medicaid provider policy; underlying code-specific rules not yet extracted

Gateway verified, not a coverage determination; fee schedule, manual and plan rules need review.

Kentucky

Warranty protection for new assistive devices

In Kentucky, a new assistive device, including manual and power wheelchairs, must have at least a one-year warranty.

Source: KRS 151B.300,.305 (from the law or rule text) · Kentucky new assistive-device warranty · Kentucky assistive-device definitions · checked 2026-11-01

Fine print

A new assistive device has a minimum one-year warranty under the act.

Who it covers: Covered new devices, including manual/power wheelchairs

Whether this applies depends on your device, seller and situation. Open the source to check the details.

Substitute equipment or rental reimbursement

In Kentucky, if a qualifying warranty repair takes more than ten working days, or the same problem has been brought in at least twice before, you can be owed a comparable substitute or rental reimbursement.

Source: KRS 151B.325 (from the law or rule text) · Kentucky substitute device during repair · checked 2026-11-01

Fine print

Comparable substitute device or rental reimbursement is required during qualifying warranty repair exceeding ten working days, or for the same problem after at least two previous repair tenders.

Who it covers: Warranty device tendered to original/exchange dealer; statutory conditions apply

Whether this applies depends on your device, seller and situation. Open the source to check the details.

Utah

Warranty scope includes a price threshold

In Utah, new assistive technology costing $1,000 or more must have at least a one-year warranty, and qualifying repairs are free for parts and labor.

Source: Utah 70A-2-802 to 804 (from the law or rule text) · Utah Assistive Technology Warranty Act · checked 2026-11-01

Fine print

Minimum one-year warranty; qualifying warranty repairs are without parts/labor charge.

Who it covers: New assistive technology with retail price at least $1,000; specified exclusions

Batteries, nonfunctional accessories and normal recurring maintenance excluded; do not generalize to lower-priced devices.

Indiana

Repair approval, warranty and rental responsibilities

Indiana Medicaid does not pay for repairs a warranty should cover, and repairs to rented equipment are the rental company’s job. Some repairs need approval first.

Source: PROMOD00024 v7.0, Repair and Servicing (from the law or rule text) · Indiana DME/HME provider manual · checked 2026-11-01

Fine print

Repair-code prior authorization depends on the fee schedule; warranty repairs are not paid by IHCP, and rental repairs are the rental provider’s responsibility.

Who it covers: Indiana Health Coverage Programs DME/HME provider manual

Manual policy date, not a statute commencement. MCE-specific implementation and code requirements remain open.

West Virginia

Assistive-device warranty requirement

In West Virginia, a qualifying assistive device must have at least a one-year warranty, and certain limits on that warranty are not allowed.

Source: WV Code 46A-6E-2 (from the law or rule text) · West Virginia assistive-device warranty · checked 2026-11-01

Fine print

At least a one-year express warranty is required; specified warranty/remedy limitations are void.

Who it covers: Covered assistive devices/consumers under article definitions

Definitions and remedy procedures in adjacent sections still need extraction.

Texas

Adaptive aids through STAR+PLUS HCBS

In Texas, people enrolled in STAR+PLUS home and community services can get mobility aids and out-of-warranty repairs through their service plan.

Source: STAR+PLUS Handbook 6400 (from the law or rule text) · Texas STAR+PLUS adaptive aids · checked 2026-11-01

Fine print

STAR+PLUS HCBS adaptive aids include mobility aids and repair/maintenance outside warranty, subject to the individual service plan.

Who it covers: STAR+PLUS HCBS participants; not all Texans

Older handbook revision; verify current MCO policy and authorization before use.

Vehicle-adaptation program pathway

Texas STAR+PLUS home and community services list vehicle modifications among the adaptive aids a service plan can include.

Source: STAR+PLUS Handbook 6400 (from the law or rule text) · Texas STAR+PLUS adaptive aids · checked 2026-11-01

Fine print

Vehicle modifications are among the described adaptive-aid categories.

Who it covers: STAR+PLUS HCBS service-plan route

Not a universal vehicle purchase or modification entitlement; current limits and approval remain to verify.

Florida

New assistive-device warranty requirement

In Florida, a new covered assistive device must have at least a one-year warranty.

Source: Fla. Stat. 427.803 (from the law or rule text) · Florida assistive technology warranty · checked 2026-11-01

Fine print

Minimum one-year express warranty applies to new covered assistive-technology devices.

Who it covers: Definitions in 427.802 and remedy procedures in 427.804 need accompanying review

Warranty existence reviewed; device exclusions and remedy deadlines not yet mapped.

California

Powered-wheelchair repair access: legislative summary

California passed a 2024 law giving access to parts, tools and repair information for powered wheelchairs sold on or after January 1, 2025.

Source: SB 1384, Chapter 797 (2024) (from an official summary of the law) · California Senate SB 1384 chaptered summary · checked 2026-11-01

Fine print

Official chaptered summary identifies powered-wheelchair parts, tools and information access on fair/reasonable terms.

Who it covers: Powered wheelchairs; detailed part/clinical exclusions not verified in current code

Summary says products sold on/after January 1, 2025. Full code applicability, including in-use equipment and clinical component limits, requires verification.

Virginia

Assistive-device warranty remedies

In Virginia, new covered assistive devices carry at least a one-year warranty. Qualifying defects are repaired free, and replacement or refund is possible after reasonable repair tries.

Source: Va. Code 59.1-470 to 471 (from the law or rule text) · Virginia Assistive Technology Device Warranties Act · checked 2026-11-01

Fine print

One-year implied warranty supplements express warranty; qualifying nonconformities are repaired without parts/labor charge, with replacement/refund remedies after reasonable repair attempts.

Who it covers: New covered assistive devices; definitions and exclusions apply

Normal wear, routine maintenance and unauthorized modifications are among exclusions; downtime rules account for loaner availability.

North Carolina

State public-place and transportation access text

North Carolina law recognizes the right of people with disabilities to equal use of public places and public transportation.

Source: N.C.G.S.168-2,168-3 (from the law or rule text) · North Carolina disability access rights · checked 2026-11-01

Fine print

State text recognizes equal use of public places and transport accommodations, subject to lawful conditions.

Who it covers: People with disabilities; actual mode/provider obligations need further review

Does not establish a door-to-door ride, a specific rural service level or a wheelchair repair right.

Colorado

Powered-wheelchair repair access: enacted summaries

Colorado requires powered-wheelchair makers to provide repair resources so owners and independent repair shops can fix them.

Source: HB22-1031; HB24-1121 (from an official summary of the law) · Colorado powered wheelchair repair act · Colorado repair-law expansion · checked 2026-11-01

Fine print

Enacted summaries establish manufacturer resources for powered-wheelchair repair by owners and independent providers; later digital-equipment expansion preserves the powered-wheelchair distinction.

Who it covers: Powered wheelchair repair; not all medical devices or all mobility aids

Full current CRS Part 15 and device-specific application must be checked before an enforcement claim.

Washington

Repair-resource access for mobility devices

Washington requires makers of wheelchairs, scooters and power assists to make repair parts, tools and information available on fair terms.

Source: 2025 Wash. Laws ch.354, sections 2-3 (from the law or rule text) · Washington mobility-equipment repair act · checked 2026-11-01

Fine print

Manufacturers must make repair resources available on fair/reasonable terms for covered mobility equipment.

Who it covers: Includes powered/manual wheelchairs, mobility scooters and manual-chair power assists

Exceptions and manufacturer availability constraints apply; payer coverage and repair completion time are separate questions.

Nevada

Repair-resource access and parts-pairing restrictions

Nevada requires mobility-device makers to provide repair resources on fair terms and limits parts-pairing that blocks repairs.

Source: 2025 Nev. Stats. ch.129, AB407 (from the law or rule text) · Nevada mobility-device repair act · checked 2026-11-01

Fine print

Manufacturers must provide repair resources on fair/reasonable terms; the act also restricts specified parts-pairing barriers.

Who it covers: Mobility devices including powered/manual wheelchairs, scooters and power assists

Effective on approval under section 33, not the October date from neighboring bills. Exceptions and civil enforcement procedures require individual review.

Connecticut

Dealer response and parts-order deadlines

In Connecticut, an authorized wheelchair dealer must respond within one business day and order parts within three business days after the assessment or insurer approval.

Source: Conn. Gen. Stat.42-338(a)-(b) (from the law or rule text) · Connecticut assistive device and wheelchair repair law · checked 2026-11-01

Fine print

Authorized wheelchair dealer must respond within one business day and order parts within three business days after assessment or insurer authorization.

Who it covers: Wheelchairs sold/leased by the dealer; CRT home-repair provision also exists

Whether this applies depends on your device, seller and situation. Open the source to check the details.

Approval restrictions for specified insurance policies

In Connecticut, some state-regulated insurance plans can’t require a new prescription or prior approval to repair or replace a complex rehab wheelchair unless the original prescription is more than five years old.

Source: Conn. Gen. Stat.38a-492w,38a-518w (from the law or rule text) · Connecticut 2026 insurance supplement · checked 2026-11-01

Fine print

Specified individual/group policies cannot require a new prescription or prior authorization for medically necessary CRT wheelchair repair/replacement unless the original prescription is older than five years.

Who it covers: Specified state-regulated health policies and CRT; not automatically Medicare, self-funded ERISA plans or every payer

Written medical-necessity determination and policy/device definitions apply. Medicaid section is not fully reconciled here.

Assistive-device defect repair protection

In Connecticut, defects in a covered assistive device must be repaired during the warranty or for two years after delivery, whichever is longer.

Source: Conn. Gen. Stat.42-330,42-331(a) (from the law or rule text) · Connecticut assistive device and wheelchair repair law · checked 2026-11-01

Fine print

Assistive-device nonconformity repair protection uses the express-warranty period or two years after delivery, whichever is longer.

Who it covers: Covered AT devices; excludes batteries/nonessential accessories

Not the same as a universal two-year manufacturer express warranty; claim reporting and availability conditions apply.
