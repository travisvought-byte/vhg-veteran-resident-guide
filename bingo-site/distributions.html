Instant bingo distributions: what Ohio law requires

A plain-English summary for post commanders, finance officers and bingo volunteers. It follows Ohio Revised Code 2915.101. It is not legal advice. When in doubt, ask the Ohio Attorney General’s Charitable Law Section.

Veteran’s, fraternal and sporting organizations

This applies to net profit from paper and electronic instant bingo (pull-tabs), each calendar year.

Up to the threshold

  • At least 25% goes to a qualifying charity (see ORC 2915.01(V)(1)) or a government agency.
  • Up to 75% may be kept for the organization’s allowed bingo expenses.

The statute sets the threshold at $250,000 and lets the Attorney General raise it for inflation. Check OAC 109:1-4-21 for the current amount.

Above the threshold

  • At least 50% goes to a qualifying charity or a government agency.
  • Up to 5% may go to the organization’s own charitable purposes or a community action agency.
  • Up to 45% may be kept for allowed expenses.

Anything not distributed or kept for those purposes must go to a qualifying charity.

Other charitable organizations

Churches, schools, booster clubs and other charities that run instant bingo must give 100% of net profit to a qualifying charity or a government agency.

Pass-through charities

The law allows a hall to give to a 501(c)(3) that makes donations to other organizations and lets donors advise where the money goes. Those donations still have to meet 501(c)(3) rules. Money that comes back to the hall for its own operations is not a charitable distribution.

What gets halls in trouble

  • Paying the contracted charity less than the required amount
  • Bingo bank accounts that don’t match the money collected
  • Paying unallowable expenses from bingo accounts
  • Missing required reports

The Attorney General posts notices, settlements and orders on its bingo enforcement page. Problems are usually caught at annual inspections or license renewal.
