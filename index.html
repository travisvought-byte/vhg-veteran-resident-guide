Need help now?

Suicide or emotional crisis: Call 988 or text 988. Veterans-specific support is available through the call service. Online crisis chat. For immediate danger, call 911 or go to the nearest emergency department.

Homeless or facing housing loss: Call 877-424-3838 for free, confidential VA housing referrals, 24/7. Family members and supporters may call too.

Other funding routes and living arrangement guidance

Before committing to work: Ask the funding agency what must be approved before a deposit, purchase or installation, which providers it accepts, and who pays any uncovered cost. Do not assume an application guarantees reimbursement.

HISA application checklist and first call

For medically necessary improvements to a primary home, ask the VA medical facility’s Prosthetic and Sensory Aids Service about HISA. A county veterans office can help with the referral; the VA clinical/prosthetics team handles medical review.

  • VA-written or approved prescription identifying the proposed change and medical justification.
  • Completed and signed VA Form 10-0103. Ask about advance payment if needed.
  • Owner authorization; a renter’s owner permission must be signed and notarized.
  • Written estimate separating labor, materials, permits and inspections.
  • Color photograph of the area before modification.
  • Confirm whether VA needs a site inspection and how to submit the complete package.

Permanent access ramps can be covered when medically justified. HISA excludes portable ramps, stair glides, routine repairs and new construction. Ask Prosthetics about a separate equipment route for removable equipment.

What to say

“I need help entering my home or using the bathroom safely. Who can assess the medical need and help me apply for HISA? What benefit remains, what must be approved before work, and how should I submit the estimate?”

Prepare an estimate that the funding agency can review

Ask for a written scope, drawing where appropriate, materials and labor breakdown, permits, inspections, completion schedule and warranty. Confirm owner consent and the agency’s provider requirements before choosing a builder. Ohio’s waiver rule specifies proposal and approval requirements; VA and other funders have their own processes.

Tell the agency about other funding or donations and ask how to coordinate them. Have each funder identify the costs it will cover and the remaining gap.

A simple handoff for staff and families

  1. Ask about military service. Was the resident a veteran, or the surviving spouse of a veteran?
  2. Offer a county office referral. With the resident’s or authorized representative’s agreement, help them contact the appropriate office.
  3. Explain the need. Ask for a benefits screening; mention nursing home care, existing benefits and any difficulty traveling. Ask about phone appointments or facility visits.
  4. Agree on follow-up. Identify who will call back and when. Keep notes in the facility’s usual system.
A useful opening for the call

“I’m helping a veteran or surviving spouse who lives in a care facility in [county]. They’d like help reviewing possible benefits. What is the intake process, what documents are needed, and what options are there if they cannot travel?”

Ask about discharge records, current VA benefit letters, care expenses and payer information. Missing records? Use the National Archives resource or ask the county office for help. Discuss private documents directly with the office.
