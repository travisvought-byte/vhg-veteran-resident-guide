Rules every hall should know

Plain-language notes. The statute controls, so follow the link and read it.

  • You need a license. Bingo requires a license from the Attorney General's Charitable Law Section. Details
  • Volunteers only. A charity may not pay its bingo game operators, and operators may not accept pay from any source. ORC 2915.09
  • No consulting or concession fees. A charity running traditional bingo may not pay consulting fees for services related to its session, or concession fees to a refreshment provider. ORC 2915.09
  • Where the money can go. Gross receipts may pay prizes, rent, bingo supplies, security, bingo advertising and the other listed expenses, at customary and reasonable amounts. ORC 2915.09
  • Session limits. No more than three sessions in seven days, with a narrow exception, and no more than $6,000 in traditional bingo prizes per session. ORC 2915.09
  • Separate bank accounts. Each type of bingo needs its own checking account. ORC 2915.10
  • Who can work the game. No one with a felony or gambling conviction may be a bingo game operator. ORC 2915.11
  • Licensed distributors only. Buy or lease bingo supplies only from a distributor licensed by the Attorney General. ORC 2915.09

This is a summary for orientation, not legal advice. For a ruling on your situation, call the Charitable Law Section at 800-282-0515 or talk with your attorney. More links are on the Ohio law page.

Why we charge for promotion and not for advice

Ohio lets a charity spend bingo receipts on advertising its game. It does not let a charity running traditional bingo pay consulting fees for services related to the session. So our paid work is promotion you can hold in your hand: flyers, game sheets, listings and outreach.

Questions about running your game? Ask. We answer them free, and we will point you to the Attorney General's forms and training.
