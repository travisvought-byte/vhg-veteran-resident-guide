MILITARY SEXUAL TRAUMA

Help after military sexual trauma

If something happened to you while you served, you are not alone, and it is not too late to get help. This page lays out your options in plain words. You choose what to do, and when.

Free care from VA, even without a rating or a report

What VA says about MST-related care

  • VA provides free treatment for any physical or mental health condition related to MST.
  • You don’t need to have reported it at the time, or have any other proof, to get this care.
  • It is open to veterans and most former service members, including many with an other than honorable discharge. The usual length-of-service rules don’t apply.
  • You don’t need a VA disability rating (service connection).
  • You don’t have to talk about MST when you sign up for VA health care.
  • You can ask to see a clinician of a particular sex if that feels more comfortable.

Current service members may need a referral from the Defense Department for some services.

Source: VA: Military sexual trauma (MST); VA National Center for PTSD: Military sexual trauma. Checked November 23, 2026.

Talk to an MST coordinator

Every VA medical facility has an MST coordinator. They are a contact person for anything related to MST and can help you get care. You can call and simply say you would like to speak with the MST coordinator.

Counseling at a Vet Center

Vet Centers are small community counseling centers, separate from VA hospitals. They offer counseling for any veteran or service member who experienced MST, including National Guard and Reserve members, whenever they served.

Vet Center Call Center: 877-927-8387, any hour

Ask to talk with someone about MST-related counseling, or to be connected with the Vet Center nearest you.

Source: Vet Centers: Military sexual trauma; VA: Military sexual trauma (MST). Checked November 23, 2026.

Disability compensation for conditions related to MST

If MST left you with a condition such as PTSD, depression or a physical injury, you can file a VA disability claim. Many people never reported what happened, and VA knows that.

Evidence VA can use (the “markers”)

Besides service and personnel records, Defense Department reports and investigations, VA accepts evidence from other sources, for example:

  • Statements from a chaplain, counselor, family member or fellow service member, or from a rape crisis center
  • Civilian police or medical records, and personal journals or diaries
  • Signs that things changed after the event: a request for a transfer, a drop in work performance, relationship trouble, more drinking or drug use, panic attacks or depression
  • Records such as pregnancy tests or tests for sexually transmitted infections

VA says this indirect evidence doesn’t need to show a clear cause. Before VA turns down a PTSD claim based on an assault, the rule requires it to tell you about these other kinds of evidence so you can send them.

Help with the claim

  • Every VA regional office has a male and a female MST outreach coordinator who can help you file, and claim processors get specialized training.
  • You can ask for a male or a female examiner for your claim exam.
  • A free accredited representative, such as a county veterans service officer, can file with you. Find claims help in your state.
  • If a claim was denied before, you can ask for a decision review.

Source: VA: Military sexual trauma and disability compensation; 38 CFR 3.304(f)(5). Checked November 23, 2026.

If MST affected your discharge

Sometimes what happened led to trouble in service and a discharge that doesn’t reflect who you are. Since 2017 the Defense Department has told its discharge review boards to give liberal consideration to upgrade requests involving mental health conditions, including those from sexual assault or sexual harassment. You can give the board evidence of a diagnosis, or evidence that you experienced the assault or harassment.

Remember: you can get MST-related care from VA now, while you wait, even with an other than honorable discharge.

VA’s discharge upgrade tool asks a few questions and tells you which form and board to use. Questions: 800-827-1000, Monday to Friday, 8 a.m. to 9 p.m. ET.

The guide’s records and discharge upgrade section has the forms and helpers. The guide’s liberal consideration page and bad paper section walk through upgrading an other than honorable discharge, step by step, with free help.

Source: Army.mil, August 30, 2017: DoD offers new policy guidance for veterans’ discharge upgrade requests; VA: Request a discharge upgrade or correction; VA: Military sexual trauma (MST). Checked November 23, 2026.

For men

MST happens to men as well as women, and many of the veterans who tell VA about MST are men. Everything on this page is for you too: the free care, the counselors, the claims help and the privacy.

  • You can ask for a male or a female clinician, and a male or a female claim examiner.
  • Every VA regional office has a male MST outreach coordinator.
  • The MST coordinators and Vet Centers above serve every veteran who experienced MST.

Source: VA National Center for PTSD: Military sexual trauma; VA: Military sexual trauma and disability compensation; VA: Military sexual trauma (MST). Checked November 23, 2026.

Crisis and urgent lines

Veterans Crisis Line: call 988 and press 1, or text 838255, any time. If you are in danger right now, call 911.

You can go at your own pace. It is okay to start with one phone call, or just to bookmark this page for later.
