Help at home

Find help at home

Repairs, accessibility changes, caregiving, and yard and snow work for veterans in Morrow and Knox counties, Ohio: what it usually costs, who may pay for it, and providers recommended by veterans, who you call directly. Free to use.

A different kind of list

Lead websites sell the same request to several businesses at once, so the price of every job carries the cost of the leads. Here, providers are people veterans already trust, and the call goes straight to them.

Home repairs

Plumbing, electrical, heating, roofs, gutters, steps and the small jobs that pile up.

For example

  • A leak or a running toilet
  • No heat or no hot water
  • A loose railing or a broken step
  • Gutters, siding and roof leaks

Who may pay

  • Aid and AttendanceCash you choose how to spend (VA benefits)
  • USDA repair loan or grantYes (U.S. Department of Agriculture)
  • Ohio Medicaid chore helpSome (Ohio Medicaid)

What each one covers

What it usually costs

Handyman company: $75 to $125 an hour (HomeGuide). More price ranges. A guide to spot a quote far out of line, not a price anyone has to charge.

Accessibility changes

Ramps, grab bars, roll-in showers, wider doors and lower counters, so you can stay in your home.

For example

  • A ramp to the front or back door
  • Grab bars by the toilet and tub
  • A roll-in or walk-in shower
  • Lower sinks and counters

Who may pay

  • HISA grantYes (VA health care)
  • SAH and SHA grantsYes (VA benefits)
  • Aid and AttendanceCash you choose how to spend (VA benefits)
  • USDA repair loan or grantYes (U.S. Department of Agriculture)

What each one covers

What it usually costs

Wheelchair ramp, installed: $100 to $250 a foot (HomeGuide, U.S. average). More price ranges. A guide to spot a quote far out of line, not a price anyone has to charge.

Providers

First providers coming. We’re asking veterans who they trust for accessibility changes near them. Recommend someone good, or, if you do this work, sign up here.

More help

  • Ramps and getting in and out of the house (Mobility Talk)

Caregiving at home

Help with bathing, dressing, meals and errands, and a break for the family member who does it every day.

For example

  • Help with bathing and dressing
  • Meals, laundry and light housework
  • A few hours so the caregiver can rest
  • Rides to appointments

Who may pay

  • Home health aideYes (VA health care)
  • Veteran-Directed CareYes (VA health care)
  • Respite careYes (VA health care)
  • Family caregiver stipendYes (VA Caregiver Support Program)
  • Aid and AttendanceYes (VA benefits)

What each one covers

What it usually costs

Home care aide (non-medical): About $35 an hour (CareScout, U.S. average). More price ranges. A guide to spot a quote far out of line, not a price anyone has to charge.

Yard and snow

Not sure where to start? Call one of these

Free, and they know what’s available where you live.

Morrow County

  • Morrow County Veterans Service Office419-946-1914
    Free help with VA benefits, including the pension that can pay for help at home. Ask what county help there is for veterans.
  • Area Agency on Aging District 5800-860-5799
    Runs Veteran-Directed Care for this area, and is the starting point for meals, rides, caregiver support and help at home for older adults.
  • Seniors on Center (Mount Gilead)419-946-4191
    Meals, home-delivered meals and rides for Morrow residents 60 and older. When we last checked, in October 2026, its homemaker help was paused for lack of staff.
  • HelpLine 211211
    Ask for local help with repairs, utilities and other needs.

Tell us how a job went

Hired someone from this page? Tell us the date, what they did, what they quoted and what you paid, and how it went. We check with the provider and ask how they made any problem right. What we can confirm goes on their listing; a pattern of problems that aren’t made right takes a listing down.
