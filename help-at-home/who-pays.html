Help at home

Who pays for help at home

The VA programs and other help that can pay for repairs, accessibility changes, caregiving, and yard and snow work, matched to each kind of help. Amounts are from each official page, read November 28, 2026.

At a glance

By kind of help

Home repairs

VA doesn’t pay for routine repairs such as a new roof or furnace. A USDA loan or grant can, if you live in a rural area and your income is low; Ohio Medicaid’s chore help covers minor repairs for people on its home care programs; and a VA pension or disability payment is yours to spend on repairs.

  • VA pension with Aid and Attendance or HouseboundCash you choose how to spend
  • USDA home repair loans and grants (Section 504)Yes
  • Ohio Medicaid home maintenance and choreSome

Accessibility changes

This is where VA pays the most. A HISA grant covers changes your VA doctor prescribes, and the adapted housing grants cover bigger projects for certain severe service-connected disabilities. You can get both.

  • HISA grant (Home Improvements and Structural Alterations)Yes
  • Adapted housing grants (SAH and SHA)Yes
  • VA pension with Aid and Attendance or HouseboundCash you choose how to spend
  • USDA home repair loans and grants (Section 504)Yes

Find help with accessibility changes

Caregiving at home

VA can send an aide, give you a budget to hire your own helpers (family and neighbors included), pay for a break for the caregiver, or pay the family caregiver a monthly stipend.

  • Homemaker and home health aide careYes
  • Veteran-Directed CareYes
  • Respite careYes
  • Family caregiver program (PCAFC)Yes
  • VA pension with Aid and Attendance or HouseboundYes

Find help with caregiving at home

Yard and snow

No VA program pays for routine yard work. Ohio Medicaid’s chore help covers yard work that isn’t routine, including snow removal, when it keeps someone safe at home. Otherwise, Aid and Attendance or another VA payment you receive can cover a mowing or snow service.

  • VA pension with Aid and Attendance or HouseboundCash you choose how to spend
  • Ohio Medicaid home maintenance and choreSome

The programs

HISA grant (Home Improvements and Structural Alterations)

From VA health care

What it pays for
Changes your VA doctor says you need for your health or to get around your home: ramps, roll-in showers, lower sinks and counters, a way in and out of the house, and electrical or plumbing work for medical equipment.
What it doesn’t
Not routine upkeep such as a new roof, furnace or air conditioner, and not portable ramps, porch lifts, stair glides, hot tubs, decks or new construction.
Who can get it
Veterans in VA health care. Up to $6,800 over your lifetime if the change is for a service-connected disability, or you are rated 50 percent or more; up to $2,000 for everyone else.
How to start
Ask your VA doctor for a HISA prescription. The application is VA Form 10-0103, with an itemized estimate and a color photo of the area to change. VA can pay part up front if you ask on the form.

Source: VA Prosthetic and Sensory Aids Service: HISA, read November 28, 2026.

Adapted housing grants (SAH and SHA)

From VA benefits

What it pays for
Bigger changes, or building or buying a home that works with a serious disability. You can use the grant up to 6 times over your lifetime.
Who can get it
Veterans and service members with certain severe service-connected disabilities, such as the loss or loss of use of limbs, blindness, or severe burns. VA’s page lists them all.
How much
The most VA will pay in fiscal year 2027. VA can change it each year with building costs.

Apply on VA.gov. A county veterans service officer can help, free. You can get a HISA grant too.

Homemaker and home health aide care

From VA health care

What it pays for
A trained aide comes to your home to help with bathing, dressing, eating, and errands such as grocery shopping. Aides are supervised by a registered nurse; they are not nurses.
What it doesn’t
VA sets up the aide through an agency it approves. To choose your own helpers, ask about Veteran-Directed Care.
Who can get it
Veterans in VA health care who meet the clinical need for it, where it is available. A copay may apply, depending on your service-connected status.
How to start
Talk with a VA social worker. They look at what you need and set it up.

Source: VA Geriatrics and Extended Care: Homemaker and Home Health Aide Care, read November 28, 2026.

Veteran-Directed Care

From VA health care

What it pays for
You get a budget for care and hire your own helpers, who can include a family member or a neighbor. A counselor helps with the hiring and paperwork.
What it doesn’t
The budget pays for approved care, not cash to spend on anything.
Who can get it
Veterans in VA health care who need help with daily activities such as bathing, dressing or fixing meals, who are isolated, or whose caregiver is worn down, where the program is offered.
How to start
Ask your VA social worker whether it is offered where you live. In Morrow and Knox counties, the Area Agency on Aging District 5 runs it: 800-860-5799.

Source: VA Geriatrics and Extended Care: Veteran-Directed Care, read November 28, 2026.

Respite care

From VA health care

What it pays for
VA pays for care for a short time so the family caregiver can rest, run errands or travel for a few days. It can be an aide at home, adult day care or a short nursing home stay.
Who can get it
Veterans in VA health care who meet the clinical need for it, where it is available. A copay may apply.
How to start
Ask your VA social worker or case manager. The form is VA Form 10-10EC, the application for extended care benefits.

Source: VA Geriatrics and Extended Care: Respite Care, read November 28, 2026.

Family caregiver program (PCAFC)

From VA Caregiver Support Program

What it pays for
A monthly payment to the family caregiver, health insurance through CHAMPVA if they have none, counseling, and at least 30 days of respite care a year.
Who can get it
Veterans with a service-connected disability rating of 70 percent or more who need in-person care for at least six months in a row.
How to start
Apply with VA Form 10-10CG. The VA Caregiver Support Line (855-260-3274) can help with the form or check on it.

Source: VA Caregiver Support Program: Program of Comprehensive Assistance for Family Caregivers, read November 28, 2026.

VA pension with Aid and Attendance or Housebound

From VA benefits

What it pays for
Extra monthly pension for wartime veterans and survivors who need help with daily activities or are mostly stuck at home. It is money you spend as you choose, so it can pay a caregiver, a handyman or someone to clear the snow. VA disability compensation is yours to spend the same way.
Who can get it
Wartime veterans and surviving spouses with limited income and savings who need the help.
How to start
A county veterans service officer can file it with you, free. Our pension page explains who qualifies and how to apply.

Source: VA.gov: Aid and Attendance benefits and Housebound allowance, read November 28, 2026.

USDA home repair loans and grants (Section 504)

From U.S. Department of Agriculture

What it pays for
Loans to repair, improve or modernize a home, and grants to fix health and safety hazards or make a home accessible. Loans go up to $40,000 at a fixed 1 percent over 20 years; grants go up to $10,000. You can combine them for up to $50,000.
What it doesn’t
Grants must be paid back if you sell the home within three years.
Who can get it
Homeowners in rural areas with household income under the limit USDA sets for their county; grants are for people 62 or older. Military service isn’t needed. Much of Morrow and Knox counties counts as rural; USDA’s map checks your address.
How to start
Call the USDA Rural Development office for Ohio, listed on the program page.

Source: USDA Rural Development: Single Family Housing Repair Loans and Grants in Ohio, read November 28, 2026.

Ohio Medicaid home maintenance and chore

From Ohio Medicaid

What it pays for
Minor home repairs, including inspecting and repairing furnaces, pest control, and yard work that isn’t routine, including snow removal, when it keeps you safe at home. Up to $10,000 a year in all.
What it doesn’t
Not routine yard work such as weekly mowing. The work must be done by a provider Ohio has approved for it.
Who can get it
People on one of Ohio Medicaid’s home care programs for those who need a nursing-home level of care. Military service isn’t needed.
How to start
Ask the Area Agency on Aging District 5 (800-860-5799) whether you qualify for a Medicaid home care program, or your case manager if you already have one.

Source: Ohio Administrative Code 5160-44-12: home maintenance and chore services, read November 28, 2026.

Free help with the paperwork

A county veterans service officer will fill out VA forms with you and file them, free. Nobody should charge you to apply for VA benefits.

  • Morrow County veterans service office: 419-946-1914
  • Knox County veterans service office: 740-393-6742
  • Every Ohio county veterans service office
  • VA Caregiver Support Line: 855-260-3274
