Fair prices

What home repairs should cost in Ohio

What plumbers, electricians, roofers and other trades usually charge, from published cost guides, and the rights Ohio law gives you before anyone starts work. Typical ranges, not quotes.

Before anyone comes to your house

Ask these on the first call

  1. What is your trip or service fee, and what does it cover? Many include the first hour of work.
  2. What is your hourly rate, and your after-hours rate?
  3. Is the price you’re giving me on the phone a range or a firm price?
  4. Will you give me a written estimate before you start?
  5. Are you licensed or registered here, and insured? Can I see proof?

Red flags

  • A firm price on the phone for damage nobody has seen yet.
  • A trip fee you only hear about when the bill comes. Ohio law says it must be told up front.
  • Travel time billed as labor on top of a trip fee that already covers it.
  • An hourly rate far above the ranges below, with no reason given.
  • Pressure to decide right now, or a large payment up front. The state warns about both.
  • A bill well over the estimate that you never agreed to.

Trip fees told up front and bills over the estimate: OAC 109:4-3-05. Pressure and large payments up front: Ohio Department of Commerce: Verifying licensed contractors and state inspectors.

Hourly rates

Is $650 an hour fair for a plumber? No published rate we found goes that high. In Columbus, plumbers charge $45 to $200 an hour in normal hours, and up to $600 for holiday emergency calls (Angi).

Pay for the worker is lower than the rate a company bills: the rate also covers the truck, insurance, licenses, office and profit. What tradespeople earn in Ohio is further down.

Water damage: why nobody can price it on the phone

Cleanup depends on how much is wet and what kind of water it is. A small area in part of one room runs $150 to $400 (HomeAdvisor). A whole 500-square-foot basement of clean water runs $2,000 to $3,000 (HomeGuide). So a $2,500 price can be fair for a flooded basement and far too high for a wet corner. Ask them to look first, then give a written estimate.

If you were overcharged

Ask the business for the itemized bill Ohio law requires. If you can’t settle it, file a complaint with the Ohio Attorney General, Consumer Protection Section: file online or call 1-800-282-0515 (Attorney General’s complaint page).

If the business is in our directory, tell us too: email us. Profiles that break our rules come down.

How these ranges were put together

Read November 27, 2026 from the cost-guide pages linked in each row (Angi, HomeGuide, HomeAdvisor, Housecall Pro, Fixr, and CareScout’s cost of care survey for home care); a table with its own date was read on that date. Columbus figures are used where the publisher printed them, and U.S. averages otherwise; no publisher printed figures for other Ohio cities. Each row is one publisher’s range as printed, not a blend. We left out a few figures that looked wrong, such as an $18 outlet replacement that leaves out the service-call minimum. Prices change, and your job may differ: use these to spot a quote that is far out of line, not as a quote.
