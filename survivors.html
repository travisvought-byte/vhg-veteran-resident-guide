GOLD STAR AND SURVIVING FAMILIES

For families who have lost a service member or veteran

We are so sorry for your loss. Here is the help that exists for you, starting with people who understand. Take what you need, when you need it.

  • Someone to talk to
  • Monthly payments
  • Help from the branch
  • Gold Star lapel buttons
  • School
  • Ohio survivor benefits
  • Other states

Someone to talk to, any hour

TAPS (Tragedy Assistance Program for Survivors)

TAPS supports people grieving someone who served in the military. Its helpline is answered at any hour, and it offers casework, grief counseling, education support, suicide loss support, peer mentors, and programs for children and young adults.

800-959-8277 (800-959-TAPS), any hour

Source: TAPS National Military Survivor Helpline; MyNavy HR: Survivor resources. Checked November 23, 2026.

Monthly payments: DIC and Survivors Pension

Two VA payments may help a surviving spouse, children or parents: Dependency and Indemnity Compensation (DIC) and Survivors Pension. The guide’s surviving spouse section explains them and how to get free help applying.

Help from the service branch, for as long as you need it

Each branch has people whose job is to support surviving families long after the funeral, whatever the cause and wherever the death happened. They can help with benefits questions, and they are also the people to call if you are not satisfied with the casualty assistance you received.

Gold Star lapel buttons

  • Gold Star Lapel Button: for the next of kin of members who died in combat or other qualifying hostile circumstances, including terrorist attacks and certain peacekeeping operations. Next of kin includes spouses (remarried or not), parents and stepparents, children and stepchildren, and brothers and sisters, including half and step siblings.
  • Next of Kin of Deceased Personnel Lapel Button: for families of service members who died in a duty status and are not eligible for the Gold Star button.

The buttons are normally presented without any application. If you are eligible and never received one, or need a replacement, send DD Form 3 to the military department listed on the form.

Gold Star Mother’s and Family’s Day is the last Sunday in September, and Gold Star Spouses Day is April 5.

Source: Military OneSource: Honoring Gold Star families; DD Form 3. Checked November 23, 2026.

School for children and spouses

Fry Scholarship

For the child or surviving spouse of a service member or member of the Selected Reserve who died on or after September 11, 2001, in the line of duty or in other qualifying ways. It can pay up to 36 months of tuition and fees, housing, and books and supplies.

Source: VA: Fry Scholarship. Checked November 23, 2026.

Survivors’ and Dependents’ Educational Assistance (DEA, Chapter 35)

For the child or spouse of a service member who died in the line of duty, or of a veteran who is permanently and totally disabled because of a service-connected disability, among other situations.

Source: VA: Survivors’ and Dependents’ Educational Assistance (DEA, Chapter 35). Checked November 23, 2026.

Applying

Both use the same form, VA Form 22-5490, online or by mail. Tell the school’s certifying official that you applied. If you qualify for both, VA’s Fry page explains how they can be combined and the limits.

Source: VA: Fry Scholarship; VA: Survivors’ and Dependents’ Educational Assistance (DEA, Chapter 35). Checked November 23, 2026.

Ohio survivor benefits

Ohio War Orphans and Severely Disabled Veterans’ Children Scholarship

Helps pay general and instructional fees at Ohio colleges for children of certain veterans who died, are missing in action or prisoners of war, or are severely disabled (for example, a service-connected rating of 60% or more). Students apply between their 16th and 25th birthdays. The application lists the service and Ohio residency rules. The current application must be postmarked by May 15, 2027.

Ohio Department of Higher Education: 614-752-9528

Source: Ohio Department of Higher Education: War Orphans Scholarship application. Checked November 23, 2026.

Ohio Safety Officers College Memorial Fund

Helps pay undergraduate and certificate tuition for the children and spouses of service members killed in the line of duty in Operation Enduring Freedom, Operation Iraqi Freedom or a combat zone designated by the President. The student must live in Ohio now (not necessarily when the death happened) and not qualify for the War Orphans Scholarship. Ask the college’s financial aid office about it.

Source: Ohio Department of Higher Education: Ohio Safety Officers College Memorial Fund, guidance memo FA 27-006 (July 2, 2026). Checked November 23, 2026.

Your county veterans service office

Every Ohio county has a veterans service office. Its accredited service officers help veterans, dependents and surviving spouses file VA claims, can provide temporary emergency financial help, and point you to other local and state resources. Their help is free.

Ohio Department of Veterans Services: 877-644-6838 (877-OHIO-VET) · Find your county office in this guide

Source: Ohio Department of Veterans Services: Benefits Resource Guide 2026. Checked November 23, 2026.

Property tax and license plates

  • If your late spouse was a veteran with a total disability rating from VA, you may qualify for Ohio’s homestead exemption on the home you own and live in. Apply with your county auditor.
  • Immediate family of a service member who died in a combat zone (spouse, parents and stepparents, brothers and sisters, children and stepchildren, and grandparents) can get Gold Star Family license plates from the Ohio BMV, free of the usual registration taxes and fees.

Source: Ohio Revised Code 323.152 (homestead reduction); Ohio Revised Code 4503.546 (Gold Star Family license plates); Ohio Department of Veterans Services: Benefits Resource Guide 2026. Checked November 23, 2026.

Other states

Every state has its own survivor benefits, such as college tuition help, property tax relief and license plates. Open your state in this guide and choose Help for a surviving spouse, or call your county or state veterans office and ask, “What survivor benefits does our state offer?”
