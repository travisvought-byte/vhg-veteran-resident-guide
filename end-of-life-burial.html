Hospice, end-of-life care and burial for veterans

Comfort care, honor and a resting place: what VA provides at the end of a veteran’s life, and how families plan ahead.

VA hospice care

Hospice is comfort care for a veteran with a terminal condition and less than 6 months to live who is no longer seeking treatment other than comfort care. It can be given at home, in a clinic or in an inpatient setting, including a VA Community Living Center. The team plans care with the veteran and family and offers grief support to the family afterward.

  • Every veteran enrolled in VA health care is eligible if they have the clinical need.
  • There are no copays for hospice care, whether VA provides it or a hospice under contract with VA.
  • VA works closely with community and home hospice agencies to provide care at home.

Ask the veteran's doctor or VA social worker for a hospice referral.

Palliative care

Palliative care eases symptoms and suffering at any stage of a serious illness, and can be combined with treatment aimed at a cure. Enrolled veterans with the clinical need are eligible; copays may apply.

We Honor Veterans

We Honor Veterans was created by VA and the National Hospice and Palliative Care Organization to help community hospices care for veterans. When choosing a hospice, ask whether it takes part and how it supports veterans and their families.

Write down the veteran's wishes while they can share them. See health care powers of attorney and VA's advance directive.

VA burial allowances (as of November 23, 2026)

VA helps pay burial and funeral costs if the veteran was not dishonorably discharged and, for example, was receiving VA pension or compensation at death, died while in VA care or a VA-contracted facility, or died from a service-connected disability.

VA burial allowance amounts
SituationAmount
Service-connected death on or after September 11, 2001Up to $2,000
Death not service connected, on or after October 1, 2026$1,032 burial allowance and $1,032 for a plot
  • The spouse, child, parent, executor, a family member or friend, or a funeral home or cemetery that paid the costs can apply, using VA Form 21P-530EZ or online.
  • A surviving spouse listed on the veteran's VA record usually does not need to apply: VA pays a set amount automatically after it learns of the death.
  • Time limit: when the death was not service connected, apply within 2 years of burial, with exceptions (no limit for plot, interment or transportation allowance, or when the veteran died under VA care).
  • VA can also pay back the cost of transporting the veteran's remains for burial in a VA national cemetery.

National and state veterans cemeteries in Ohio

Burial in a VA national cemetery is open to veterans who met the minimum active duty requirement and were not dishonorably discharged. A spouse, a minor child and, in some cases, an unmarried adult child with a disability may also be eligible.

  • Ohio Western Reserve National Cemetery, 10175 Rawiga Road, Seville, OH 44273 · 330-335-3069
  • Dayton National Cemetery, 4400 West Third Street, Dayton, OH 45428 · 937-268-2221
  • Ohio Veterans Home Cemetery, 3416 Columbus Ave, Sandusky, OH 44870 · 419-625-2454 · Ohio's state veterans cemetery, built with VA grant funds

To schedule a burial in a national cemetery at the time of need, the family or funeral home faxes the discharge papers to the National Cemetery Scheduling Office at 866-900-6417 and calls 800-535-1117. Outside Ohio, find a national or state veterans cemetery.

Ohio counties also have a duty to help: the county veterans service commission arranges a decent burial for an Ohio veteran, or the veteran's spouse, surviving spouse or parent, who dies without the means to pay. Ask the county veterans office.

Plan ahead: pre-need eligibility

A veteran or family member can ask VA ahead of time whether they are eligible for burial in a VA national cemetery. A decision in advance saves the family a step later.

  • Apply online, or by mail or fax with VA Form 40-10007. By mail: NCA Evidence Intake Center, PO Box 5237, Janesville, WI 53547.
  • Include the DD214 if you have it. Someone applying for a person who cannot sign, such as a caregiver, a spouse, a court-appointed representative, someone with durable power of attorney or the head of the care facility, needs supporting papers and VA Form 21-22 or 21-22a.
  • Name a preferred national cemetery. VA cannot guarantee it, but will help the family choose another if needed.
  • State, territory and tribal veterans cemeteries have their own pre-need programs; contact the cemetery.

When no family claims the veteran's remains

An unclaimed veteran can be buried in a VA national cemetery or a VA-funded state or tribal veterans cemetery, and whoever handles the burial can get help with the costs:

  • Casket or urn allowance: $1,394 for a casket or $161 for an urn that meets VA's standards, for burial in a national cemetery or a VA-funded state or tribal veterans cemetery when the veteran had no identifiable next of kin and not enough resources. Use VA Form 40-10088.
  • Burial allowance, plot allowance and transportation to a national cemetery: VA Form 21P-530, marked “unclaimed remains.”

The Missing in America Project, a nonprofit veterans organization, works with private, state and federal partners to locate, identify and inter the unclaimed cremated remains of veterans.

What to do next

If you are the veteran

You can apply now for a pre-need decision and tell your family which cemetery you want. If hospice is right for you, it costs nothing in copays through VA.

If you are the spouse

Keep the DD214 and marriage certificate where you can find them. If your spouse received VA pension or compensation, VA usually pays the burial amount to you automatically.

If you are an adult son or daughter

Ask the funeral home to schedule a national cemetery burial and to file for the allowance. Ask the county veterans office about county burial help.

If you are a social worker or facility staff

Ask early whether the resident is enrolled in VA health care and has a pre-need decision. If a veteran dies with no family, tell the funeral home about VA’s unclaimed-veteran benefits and the Missing in America Project.
