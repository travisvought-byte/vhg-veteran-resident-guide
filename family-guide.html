Words you'll hear

Show plain-language meanings
Evaluation
Testing to see if your child has a delay or disability and what help they need. Early intervention and public schools do this for free.
Early intervention
Free help for children under 3, usually at home, such as speech or physical therapy and coaching for parents.
IEP (Individualized Education Program)
A written school plan for a child who needs special education. It lists goals, services such as speech therapy, and supports. Parents are part of the team that writes it.
504 plan
A written school plan for a child with a disability who does not need special education but needs changes to learn, such as extra test time or breaks. A child who does not qualify for an IEP may still get a 504 plan.
ABA (applied behavior analysis)
A therapy often used for autism that teaches skills step by step. Insurance, Medicaid and TRICARE rules for it vary.
County board of DD
In Ohio, the county office for developmental disabilities. It decides who qualifies and helps plan and pay for services.
Medicaid waiver
A Medicaid program that pays for help at home and in the community, such as aides or respite, for people who qualify.
SSI
Supplemental Security Income: monthly payments from Social Security for people with disabilities who have low income and savings.
ABLE / STABLE account
A savings account for a person with a disability that does not count against SSI or Medicaid limits.
Guardianship
A court gives another adult the right to make decisions for a person 18 or older. It removes some of that person's rights, so less restrictive options are worth looking at first.
Supported decision-making
The young adult keeps their rights and chooses trusted people to help them understand choices and make decisions.
Transition plan
The part of an IEP, starting by age 14 in Ohio, that plans for life after high school: work, school and living.
Respite
Short-term care that gives family caregivers a break.
EFMP
The military's Exceptional Family Member Program for active duty families with special medical or school needs.

What's changing (checked 2026-11-08)

Federal special education oversight is moving

In June 2026 the U.S. Department of Education announced it is moving day-to-day oversight of special education to the Department of Health and Human Services, and civil rights enforcement to the Department of Justice. The law has not changed: your child still has the right to a free public education and an IEP, and your school district and state are still responsible.

Sources: The federal role in special education: 5 questions (June 24, 2026) · Education Department shifting some responsibility for special education programs (June 17, 2026)

TRICARE autism coverage is under review

A national study recommended making ABA therapy a regular TRICARE benefit instead of a pilot program, and senators have asked the Defense Department to act. Check TRICARE's page for the current rules before you sign up.

Sources: Senators' letter on the National Academies ABA study (June 26, 2026) · TRICARE Autism Care Demonstration

Ohio may recognize supported decision-making in law

Senate Bill 35 would recognize supported decision-making as an alternative to guardianship for adults with developmental disabilities. The Ohio Senate passed it unanimously; it is now in a House committee. Supported decision-making can already be used today. The bill would add a standard state form and legal protections.

Sources: Ohio Senate Bill 35 (136th General Assembly) · Senate passes supported decision-making bill

    Good to know

    • If your child is 3 or older, ask your public school district in writing for a free evaluation, even if your child goes to a private school or is homeschooled. Keep a copy and write down the date.
    • A doctor's diagnosis and school services are separate decisions. A child can get an IEP or 504 plan without a particular diagnosis.
    • For a medical evaluation, ask your child's doctor for a referral to a developmental pediatrics or autism clinic at a children's hospital. Waits can be long, so ask early and get on more than one list.
    • This guide lists free public programs and nonprofits. It does not list private therapy providers. Being listed does not mean VHG endorses or partners with a program.
    • Contacts come from published sources and were not checked by phone. If something is wrong, use "Sources and corrections" on the listing.
