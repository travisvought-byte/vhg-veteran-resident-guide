Need help right now?

If your child or anyone in your family is in crisis, call or text 988, any time. If someone is in danger, call 911.

In Ohio, for anyone 20 or younger, ask 988 for MRSS: free crisis help for your child. On weekdays from 8 a.m. to 8 p.m., a team usually comes within an hour. At other times, a counselor helps by phone or video and a team visits the next day.

Military and veteran families

Active duty families have programs through the Defense Department and TRICARE. Veterans' children may qualify for VA health coverage. A county veterans service officer can help with VA paperwork, including for an adult child who has been disabled since childhood. Find your county veterans service office.

    Good to know

    • If your child is 3 or older, ask your public school district in writing for a free evaluation, even if your child goes to a private school or is homeschooled. Keep a copy and write down the date.
    • A doctor's diagnosis and school services are separate decisions. A child can get an IEP or 504 plan without a particular diagnosis.
    • For a medical evaluation, ask your child's doctor for a referral to a developmental pediatrics or autism clinic at a children's hospital. Waits can be long, so ask early and get on more than one list.
    • This guide lists free public programs and nonprofits. It does not list private therapy providers. Being listed does not mean VHG endorses or partners with a program.
    • Contacts come from published sources and were not checked by phone. Ohio's first-call numbers were matched against each organization's own website on __REVIEWED__. If something is wrong, use "Sources and corrections" on the listing.
