Who does this for free

County veterans service offices and accredited service officers

Your county veterans office and veterans service organization representatives help with VA claims, including the character of discharge review that comes with one. VA says the services an accredited VSO representative provides on your VA claim are always free (VA). Find one with VA’s representative search, or open your state in the guide and choose your county. The Navy’s review board names the American Legion and Disabled American Veterans as organizations that give guidance on upgrades (NDRB). Ask any office directly whether it helps with discharge upgrades.

Legal aid

Civil legal aid is free for people who qualify. Find the program for your area with the Legal Services Corporation’s legal aid finder, and ask whether it takes discharge upgrades or VA character of discharge cases.

National pro bono programs

  • NVLSP Lawyers Serving Warriors gives pro bono help, at no cost to veterans, with discharge upgrades and other Defense Department matters, for veterans of all eras located anywhere. Apply online, or call 202-265-8305, option 5, and leave a detailed message (NVLSP).
  • The Veterans Consortium Discharge Upgrade Program connects veterans with volunteer attorneys for other-than-honorable discharges tied to PTSD, TBI, military sexual trauma or Don’t Ask, Don’t Tell. For mental health conditions it requires a diagnosis before you apply. Apply through its online intake form. People who do not meet its criteria get referrals and self-help information (The Veterans Consortium).

Law school veterans clinics

Some law schools run veterans clinics where supervised students take discharge upgrade cases at no charge. Ask the law schools near you, or ask legal aid for a referral. Near Ohio: the Northern Kentucky University clinic serving the Greater Cincinnati region.

Self-help information

Stateside Legal is a free legal information site for veterans and service members, including discharge upgrades. Ohio Legal Help points veterans to it (Ohio Legal Help).

Your county veterans service office

Every Ohio county has a County Veterans Service Office. ODVS says they help with federal VA claims, are staffed by accredited service officers, and are free (ODVS Benefits Resource Guide). Find yours in the Ohio guide: choose your county, or browse the county office list.

Legal aid in Ohio

  • The Legal Aid Society of Columbus has a Veterans Team that lists applications to upgrade or correct a DD-214 among the issues it handles, and may represent veterans with an other-than-honorable discharge or lower who want VA benefits. It serves Central and Southeast Ohio. Main office: 614-221-7201 (LASCO).
  • Elsewhere in Ohio: find your county’s legal aid program at Ohio Legal Aids and ask whether it handles discharge upgrades.
  • Greater Cincinnati region: Northern Kentucky University’s Chase College of Law runs a Veterans Discharge Upgrade Review Clinic, where students help veterans apply for upgrades. To apply, call 859-572-6918 (NKU Chase).

Paid consultants: what to watch for

People who are not VA-accredited sell “claims help” and “upgrade services” to veterans. Know the rules before anyone asks for money.

  • On a VA claim, help from an accredited VSO representative is always free. An accredited attorney or claims agent may charge only after VA has decided your initial claim, and only with a fee agreement you both signed that VA has received, plus VA Form 21-22a appointing them (VA). Anyone charging you to start a claim, or who is not accredited, is outside those rules.
  • Check accreditation with VA’s search before you sign anything. If an accredited representative mistreats you, you can complain to VA’s Office of General Counsel (VA).
  • At the military boards you do not need anyone. The Navy’s review board says a counsel or representative is not required, and that any representation costs are paid by you and not reimbursed even if you win (NDRB). Free help exists for this; look there first.
  • Records are free. The National Archives provides DD-214s to veterans free and warns that some companies charge for them (National Archives).
  • Walk away from anyone who promises a result, wants payment before doing the work, or asks for your VA.gov, ID.me or bank login.
