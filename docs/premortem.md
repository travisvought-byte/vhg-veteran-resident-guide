# Pre-mortem

Assume it is a year from now and the project failed. These are the most likely reasons, ranked by how much damage each would do, with the countermeasure built into the plan.

| # | Failure | Why it would happen | Countermeasure | Where it is handled |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Wrong contact sends a veteran to a dead end** | County office addresses already disagree across official sources; offices move and staff change | Official sources only; source and review date on every listing; “Report a problem” on every card; fix reported errors promptly; quarterly source recheck | R-01, Stage 3, maintenance |
| 2 | **VHG or facility staff drift into claims assistance** | Well-meaning staff fill out forms or advise on eligibility; accreditation rules restrict who may help prepare VA claims | Written boundary statement; guide says "route to county office" at every decision point; attorney review before release | R-03, Stage 2 |
| 3 | **Resident information ends up somewhere it should not** | Staff email VHG about specific residents; Guardians keep notes; a tracker gets committed to the repository | No resident data anywhere in the project; handoff goes resident/family or facility directly to the county office; repository private | Boundaries §5, D-011 |
| 4 | **Facilities never use it** | Social workers believe they already know the resources; another handout goes in a drawer | Build the staff guide around the admission routine; test with two facility staff before printing; keep it to one page | Stage 4 |
| 5 | **It becomes a full disability directory again and stalls** | The broad need is real and tempting; inventory work never ends | General resources stay a link layer with one line of guidance each; expansion only by decision | Scope §4, D-009 |
| 6 | **County offices push back** | Unexpected referral volume, wrong documents, or a sense that VHG is speaking for them | Contact the four offices first; ask how they want referrals; let them review their own entry | R-01, first outreach |
| 7 | **AI-assisted research publishes something false** | A plausible but wrong phone number, program or amount passes review | Every field cites a source; search extracts marked weaker than full-page review; human confirmation before publication | Schema D-005, R-tasks |
| 8 | **Bad actors exploit the same population** | Fee-charging "pension advisers" target nursing home residents; a guide that raises benefits without a warning helps them | Staff guide includes a plain warning and points only to free, accredited help | R-03, staff guide |
| 9 | **Single-person dependency** | Travis carries maintenance alone; it lapses during busy periods | Named maintenance owner and backup; Guardians report corrections from visits; simple data files anyone can update | Stage 5 |
| 10 | **Perceived conflict of interest** | Facilities read the guide as VHG marketing; VHG lists itself among resources | VHG appears as publisher, separately labeled; no ranking or endorsement of facilities | Boundaries §5 |
| 11 | **Facility compliance blocks distribution** | Corporate operators restrict outside materials | Ask each administrator first; offer as a free, sourced reference with no data collection | Stage 5 |
| 12 | **Benefit information goes stale** | Pension limits and program rules change yearly | Amounts carry their effective period; staff guide names topics and routes, not dollar figures, unless current and dated | Schema, R-02 |

## Early warning signs

- A county office says it has never heard of the project when a resident arrives.
- Facility staff ask VHG eligibility questions instead of calling the county office.
- Any message to VHG contains a resident's name and condition.
- The task backlog grows faster than records are verified.
- A listing passes its review date without recheck.
