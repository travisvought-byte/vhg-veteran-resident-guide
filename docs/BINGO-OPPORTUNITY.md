# Where the charitable bingo money is

Travis's direction (November 20, 2026): Ohio cannot be the only, or even the best, opportunity. Rank the
states by the money charitable gaming raises, then build findbingo.org out in that order.

The partnership model is the same everywhere: free listings and promotion for every hall, and halls that
want more help name a partner charity for a share of the money they already have to give away (in Ohio,
Veteran Home Guardians, about 5 to 10 percent of the required distribution). Outside Ohio, local
organizations come first as partners (see BINGO.md).

## Where the money has to go

This decides whether a hall can name a partner charity at all.

- **Ohio.** Veteran's, fraternal and sporting organizations must distribute at least 25 percent of their
  first $330,000 of instant bingo net profit, and at least 50 percent above that, to an outside charitable
  organization or a government unit (Ohio Adm.Code 109:1-4-21, ORC 2915.101). Other charities keep all of
  it for their own purposes. This required distribution is the money the VHG partnership asks a share of.
- **Kentucky.** Licensed organizations are themselves the charities: they must keep at least 40 percent of
  adjusted gross receipts for their own charitable purposes (KRS 238.536). There is no required gift to an
  outside charity, so a Kentucky partnership would be a paid service or advertising arrangement, not a
  share of required giving.
- **Every other state:** not checked yet. Check the distribution rule before offering a partnership there.

## Ohio prospect data

The Ohio AG's yearly bingo financials list every licensed organization with receipts, prizes, expenses
and net profit by license type. Parsed on November 20, 2026 (totals match the report's own grand totals):
932 veteran, fraternal and sporting organizations had instant bingo (Type III) profit; the median required
distribution was about $55,000 a year. Groups were sorted by name, so the split is approximate.

Minnesota publishes the same kind of organization-by-organization report (linked above), which makes it
the next place to build a prospect list.

## Build order for findbingo.org

1. **Minnesota.** Largest market. "Find Gaming Sites" (https://mn.gov/gcb/data/) sends automated readers to
   a captcha, so read it in an ordinary browser and check whether it says which sites run bingo. Most
   Minnesota money is pull-tabs in bars, so bingo listings may be a small part of it.
2. **Kentucky.** Second-biggest per-person market, and today a starter state with few listings. There is no
   current statewide list: the June 2023 facility list predates House Bill 904's license transfers. Big
   halls publish their own weekly sessions; leads to read from the hall's own page (not directory sites):
   J-Town Bingo Hall (9125 Galene Dr, Jeffersontown), Bingo City at Breckenridge (3441 Breckenridge Ln,
   Louisville), Charity Hall Bingo (5001 Stephan Dr, Louisville), Big Blue Bingo Hall (3560 New Hartford
   Rd, Owensboro).
3. **New Hampshire.** The Lottery Commission publishes licensed commercial bingo halls by town (Claremont,
   Concord, Dover, Hudson, Keene, Laconia, Manchester, Nashua) at
   https://compliance.lottery.nh.gov/resources-guidance/commercial-bingo-hall-locations. The site turns
   away automated readers (Access Denied), so read it in an ordinary browser. It would make New Hampshire a
   `licenses` state.
4. **North Dakota.** Large, but its sites list is mostly bars selling pull-tabs with no column for bingo
   (see FINDBINGO.md).
5. **Indiana.** Licenses are loaded; game days and times are the gap.

Do not try to get around a site that blocks automated readers; read it in a browser.
